000035 | 2. Smallest decisive spectral-heat covariance test
000036 | Fix a single spatial frequency with omega>=0 and geometric time tau on R:
000037 |   K=-partial_tau^2+omega^2,
000038 |   C_s=exp(-s K), s>0.
000039 | This is a positive covariance operator with a positive point Laplace measure
000040 | at spectral heat parameter s. Its translation kernel is real, positive and
000041 | invariant under tau reflection:
000042 |   c_s(u)=exp(-s omega^2)(4pi s)^(-1/2)exp(-u^2/(4s)).
000043 | The reflected covariance on positive times a,b is c_s(a+b), not c_s(a-b).
000044 | For any distinct a,b>0 its2x2 Gram determinant has ratio
000045 |   c_s(2a)c_s(2b)/c_s(a+b)^2
000046 |     =exp(-(a-b)^2/(2s))<1.
000047 | Thus the determinant is strictly negative despite covariance positivity and
000048 | reflection invariance. For example s=1,a=1,b=2, set
000049 |   d=exp(-omega^2)/(2sqrt(pi)),
000050 |   f=delta_1-exp(7/4)delta_2.
000051 | Then its reflected quadratic form is exactly
000052 |   q(f)=d[exp(-1)-exp(-1/2)]<0.
000053 | The deltas can be replaced by smooth real mollifiers supported in small
000054 | neighborhoods of1 and2 inside tau>0; smoothness of c_s preserves the strict
000055 | negative sign. Hence this is a genuine supported-test-function obstruction,
000056 | not an artifact of allowing delta insertions.
000057 | 
000058 | This one atom already disproves sufficiency of an arbitrary positive Laplace
000059 | measure for time RP of f(K). It does NOT assert that every mixture fails:
000060 | integrating heat kernels with suitable weights can yield an RP covariance.
000061 | It is not a complete P1/P2 or admissibility countermodel.

000063 | 3. The whole continuous smooth scalar covariance class
000064 | There is an elementary stronger class test, not a new general mathematical
000065 | theorem. Let c:R->R be continuous, even, ordinarily positive definite, with
000066 | c(0)>0. Assume its positive-time Hankel matrices [c(t_i+t_j)] are PSD and
000067 | c is C1 at0. Then c is constant.
000068 | 
000069 | Proof: continuity extends the Hankel positivity to a time0 entry and gives
000070 |   c(0)c(2t)>=c(t)^2, t>=0.
000071 | Ordinary PD gives |c(t)|<=c(0). Put d(t)=1-c(t)/c(0)>=0. The displayed
000072 | Hankel inequality becomes
000073 |   d(2t)<=2d(t)-d(t)^2<=2d(t).
000074 | Iterating yields d(t)<=2^n d(t/2^n). Evenness and C1 at0 imply c'(0)=0,
000075 | hence d(u)=o(u). For each fixed t the right side therefore tends to0,
000076 | so d(t)=0 globally. Evenness covers negative t. Conversely a nonnegative
000077 | constant covariance plainly meets both positivity conditions.
000078 | 
000079 | Thus no nonconstant continuous scalar translation-invariant time covariance
000080 | that is differentiable through0 can have both ordinary PD and time RP. In
000081 | particular this applies to any genuinely time-smoothed Gaussian covariance
000082 | with these hypotheses; no scan over individual positive heat weights is
000083 | needed. It does NOT apply to distribution/contact kernels, infinite c(0),
000084 | nontranslation-invariant kernels, nontrivial reflection matrices or graded
000085 | fermionic kernels. Smoothing only spatial coordinates does not imply C1
000086 | through geometric time0. The usual free covariance exp(-E|t|)/(2E) has
000087 | precisely a time cusp at0 and is not excluded. Positive heat mixtures which
000088 | produce such a cusp remain compatible with the sufficient criterion below.
