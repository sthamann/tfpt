000236 | 8. Finite sequential data admit a classical history realization
000237 | 
000238 | THEOREM (standard constructive representation, no novelty claim). Given
000239 | finite preparation, action and report sets, horizon H, and nonnegative
000240 | normalized sequential probability tables p(a_1...a_n | q_1...q_n), n<=H,
000241 | suppose summing over the latest report recovers the prefix table and earlier
000242 | marginals do not depend on later choices. Then a finite classical stochastic
000243 | history machine realizes every table, including adaptive protocols.
000244 | 
000245 | Proof: a machine state is the preparation label and full history
000246 | h=(q_1,a_1,...,q_k,a_k). When q is chosen, output a with conditional weight
000247 | p(h,q,a)/p(h), then append (q,a) to h. For unreachable zero-probability h,
000248 | choose any normalized conditional. At horizon H halt. Induction telescopes
000249 | the product of conditionals to the given table. There are finitely many
000250 | histories, bounded by |preparations| sum_(k=0)^H (|actions||reports|)^k.
000251 | The model may depend on the entire history and preparation context. For
000252 | support data use permitted branches in place of positive probabilities,
000253 | assuming analogous prefix extension consistency.
000254 | 
000255 | This gives more than the initial definitions: arbitrary finite tables in
000256 | this class have an explicit classical representation. It explains an
000257 | identification LIMIT, not the origin of the physical carrier. It is not a
000258 | claim of an efficient simulator, bounded stationary memory, reversible
000259 | dynamics, preparation noncontextuality or spacelike locality. The prefix
000260 | condition supplies a direction to the experiment; it does not derive
000261 | physical causality or forbid feedback with memory. Constraints on resources,
000262 | independent preparations, causal access or operational equivalences can
000263 | make classical competitors testable; do not drop those constraints when
000264 | interpreting such a test. Nothing here overturns Bell or contextuality tests.
000265 | 
000266 | Finite sequential data can distinguish I from D, yet cannot on these
000267 | assumptions exclude ALL classical hidden-memory implementations of D.
000268 | Those are different identification questions. Finite precision also cannot
000269 | prove exact all-context equality of two empirical preparation procedures.
