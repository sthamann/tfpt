000012 | \begin{lemma}[Finite admissible hexagon gap]
000013 | \label{lem:finite-admissible-hexagon-gap}
000014 | On the admissible cusp set
000015 | \[
000016 | y\in\left\{1,\frac13,\frac23\right\},
000017 | \]
000018 | the smallest singular value of the unregulated hexagon denominator is attained at $y=\frac23$ and $n=0$. Equivalently,
000019 | \[
000020 | \epsilon_f
000021 | :=
000022 | \min_{\substack{y\in\{1,\frac13,\frac23\}\\ n=0,\dots,5}}
000023 | \left|y-\delta_{\mathrm{ph}}e^{in\pi/3}\right|
000024 | =
000025 | \frac23-\delta_{\mathrm{ph}}.
000026 | \]
000027 | Hence the finite admissible hexagon has a strictly positive spectral gap $\epsilon_f$.
000028 | \end{lemma}

000031 | For $\varepsilon=0$, the eigenvalues in \Cref{eq:transport-spectrum} reduce to
000032 | $|y-\delta_{\mathrm{ph}}e^{in\pi/3}|^{-2}$. By \Cref{thm:transport-pole-algebraic},
000033 | $\delta_{\mathrm{ph}}$ is the unique admissible root in $(1/3,2/3)$. Hence the positive-real cusp
000034 | $y=2/3$ with $n=0$ is the nearest admissible point, while every other cusp or nonzero phase has
000035 | strictly larger distance. Therefore
000036 | \[
000037 | \epsilon_f=\frac23-\delta_{\mathrm{ph}},
000038 | \]
000039 | which is positive because $\delta_{\mathrm{ph}}<2/3$.
