000209 | 5. Finite current cutoffs are finite original CAR words
000210 | -------------------------------------------------------
000211 | 
000212 | In the filled-sea representation each eigenchannel current mode has the
000213 | standard fermionic form
000214 | 
000215 |   j^a_n=sum_r :chi^a_r* chi^a_(r+n):_sea.                 (16)
000216 | 
000217 | For fixed n and fixed input cutoff P_E, only finitely many summands in (16)
000218 | act. Far from the Fermi surface both occupations agree with the sea and the
000219 | normal-ordered bilinear annihilates the vector. Every excitation in P_E uses
000220 | one of finitely many labels, and a shift by n enlarges that label window only
000221 | by |n|. Thus j^a_n P_E is a finite sum of original CAR bilinears.
000222 | 
000223 | Equations (4) and (5) show more. For fixed m and E, only finitely many current
000224 | mode tuples in (3) act, and every surviving current reduces on P_E to finitely
000225 | many CAR bilinears. This observation alone does not yet define an acceptable
000226 | microscopic approximant: P_E is a comparison projection, not part of the
000227 | physical operator.
000228 | 
000229 | Define instead a global finite continuum operator G_K(rho), K>=2, without
000230 | using E or P_E. Keep only
000231 | 
000232 |   |m|<=K for the output field mode,
000233 |   |n_i|<=K^2 for every internal current index,
000234 |   |r|<=K^4 in every CAR sum (16),                         (16a)
000235 | 
000236 | and use the same sea normal ordering, coefficients, b insertions and rhohat_m.
000237 | This is a finite sum of globally defined original CAR words of fermion degree
000238 | at most sixteen. Its one-particle entries are finite Fourier sums and hence
000239 | smooth. For each fixed E, equations (4)--(5) bound every internal current
000240 | index that can act by E+K. The source-energy bound also confines all fermion
000241 | excitation labels. Consequently there is K_E such that, for K>=K_E,
000242 | 
000243 |   G_K(rho) P_E = F8^[K](rho) P_E.                         (16b)
000244 | 
000245 | Thus G_K is independent of E, while P_E is used only to state eventual
000246 | agreement and convergence.
000247 | 
000248 | Replace each elementary continuum CAR Fourier mode in G_K by the corresponding
000249 | actual top-row QWZ field Psi_N(g), retaining the same filled-sea ordering and
000250 | adjoint. Denote this globally defined finite microscopic Fock operator by
000251 | G_(N,K)(rho). The existing microscopic theorem gives for each fixed E,K
000252 | 
000253 |   ||(G_(N,K)(rho)V_N-V_N G_K(rho))P_E|| ->0,              (17)
000254 | 
000255 | and the analogous statement for the adjoint. The original theorem is stated
000256 | vectorwise. Since P_E is finite dimensional, convergence on a basis upgrades
000257 | it to the restricted operator norm in (17).
000258 | 
000259 | Combining (14) and (17), using that V_N is a contraction, gives the transported
000260 | iterated limit
000261 | 
000262 |   lim_(K->infinity) limsup_(N->infinity)
000263 |    ||(G_(N,K)(rho)V_N-V_N F8(rho))P_E||=0,                 (18)
000264 | 
000265 | and the identical conclusion for the adjoint. Equation (18) is the repaired
000266 | strong microscopic smeared-composite theorem.
000267 | 
000268 | No K-uniform microscopic estimate was used. Therefore (18) does not assert a
000269 | simultaneous N,K limit. The finite K approximants are Fourier-mode operators
000270 | with global circle support. Equation (18) alone does not prove exact interval
000271 | support for the microscopic approximants.

000273 | 6. Separately mollified local route
000274 | -----------------------------------
000275 | 
000276 | The original current point-splitting prescription can also be retained
000277 | locally, but every elementary fermion must first be separately smeared.
000278 | 
000279 | Choose a nonnegative smooth compactly supported mollifier eta with integral
000280 | one. Then ||eta||_1=1 and therefore |etahat(sigma r)|<=1. Let
000281 | 
000282 |   eta_(sigma,x)(y)=sigma^(-1) eta((y-x)/sigma),
000283 |   psi_sigma(x)=a(eta_(sigma,x)).                           (19)
000284 | 
000285 | Use translated copies of (19) at every point in the epsilon-split definition
000286 | of the currents and the octic. Let
000287 | 
000288 |   F8_(epsilon,sigma)(rho)
000289 | 
000290 | be the result after the same centre smearing rho and the same old sea
000291 | subtractions and derivative counterterms. At finite N the subtraction may be
000292 | written with the actual microscopic filled-sea contraction; at fixed
000293 | sigma,epsilon it converges to the prescribed continuum subtraction. For each
000294 | elementary bilinear the two subtractions differ by a vanishing scalar. After
000295 | the finite Wick expansion of the octic, the complete difference is therefore
000296 | a finite sum of lower-degree CAR words whose scalar coefficients vanish as
000297 | N->infinity; it is not in general one scalar multiple of the identity. At
000298 | sigma,epsilon>0 its kernel in the at most sixteen fermion variables is smooth.
000299 | Its tensor Fourier series is absolutely summable, so it is a norm-convergent
000300 | sum of bounded finite CAR words. The fixed-regulator QWZ limit follows
000301 | termwise from the elementary theorem, with the absolute Fourier sum providing
000302 | domination. More explicitly, if c_(r_1,...,r_s) are the tensor Fourier
000303 | coefficients of one fixed-regulator kernel, then
000304 | 
000305 |   sum_(r_1,...,r_s) |c_(r_1,...,r_s)| < infinity.         (19a)
000306 | 
000307 | For a Fourier basis vector e_r, the source estimate is S_0(e_r)=1, hence
000308 | 
000309 |   ||Psi_N(e_r)||=||R_N e_r||<=1
000310 | 
000311 | uniformly in N. Every corresponding CAR word therefore has norm at most one.
000312 | Equation (19a) is an N-independent Weierstrass majorant, so termwise QWZ
000313 | convergence passes through the infinite Fourier-word sum. Thus
000314 | 
000315 |   F8_(N,epsilon,sigma)(rho)V_N psi
000316 |     ->V_N F8_(epsilon,sigma)(rho)psi                      (20)
000317 | 
000318 | for psi in D_fin, and likewise for the adjoint.
000319 | 
000320 | It remains to remove sigma and epsilon in the continuum. Wick expansion of a
000321 | degree-eight polynomial in bilinear currents is finite. It has the form
000322 | 
000323 |   sum_lambda c_lambda
000324 |     :product_(l=1)^s_lambda partial^(q_lambda,l) psi^#_l:,
000325 | 
000326 |   s_lambda<=16,
000327 |   sum_l q_lambda,l+s_lambda/2=8,                           (21)
000328 | 
000329 | including lower fermion degrees generated by the prescribed contractions.
000330 | This is precisely why bosonic current normal ordering is not the naive
000331 | degree-sixteen fermion monomial.
000332 | 
000333 | The common-counterterm hypothesis used here is exact and limited. There is
000334 | one finite universal Wick polynomial W_(epsilon,sigma)[C] in the elementary
000335 | fields, the quasifree contraction C and its prescribed divided differences.
000336 | The microscopic expression evaluates it at the actual sea contraction C_N;
000337 | the continuum expression evaluates the same polynomial, with the same
000338 | coefficients and ordering, at C. At fixed sigma,epsilon all finitely many
000339 | mollified contractions and divided differences required by W converge from
000340 | C_N to C. Expanding the finite polynomial then gives the lower-degree
000341 | vanishing terms described above. If the two sides used independently chosen
000342 | subtraction constants rather than evaluations of this same Wick polynomial,
000343 | equation (20) would not follow. The retained current point-splitting
000344 | prescription supplies precisely this common W_(epsilon,sigma)[C]; it is a
000345 | premise of the local route, not a consequence of the energy estimate.
000346 | 
000347 | The subtraction terms must be combined into (21), or equivalently into
000348 | finite divided differences, before estimating them. For every fermion mode r,
000349 | 
000350 |   |exp(i r epsilon)|=1,
000351 |   |(exp(i r epsilon)-1)/epsilon|<=|r|,                     (22)
000352 | 
000353 | and the q-th divided difference is bounded by |r|^q. Mollifier multipliers
000354 | obey |etahat(sigma r)|<=1 and converge pointwise to one.
000355 | 
000356 | On P_E each fixed total field mode has only finitely many acting fermion-mode
000357 | tuples. Equations (21) and (22) give termwise convergence as sigma->0 and then
000358 | epsilon->0. They also give a polynomial majorant uniform in small sigma and
000359 | epsilon. A conservative count is
000360 | 
000361 |   number of tuples: O((1+E+|m|)^(s_lambda-1)),
000362 |   derivative weight: O((1+E+|m|)^(8-s_lambda/2)),
000363 | 
000364 | whose combined exponent is
000365 | 
000366 |   s_lambda/2+7 <=15.                                      (23)
000367 | 
000368 | Smooth rho absorbs the power fifteen just as in (11). Dominated summation over
000369 | m now yields, on every P_E,
000370 | 
000371 |   strong-lim_(epsilon->0) strong-lim_(sigma->0)
000372 |     F8_(epsilon,sigma)(rho)=F8(rho),                       (24)
000373 | 
000374 | and the same limit for the adjoint. The target in (24) is the mode-defined
000375 | F8(rho) of (12), because the retained point-splitting counterterms are exactly
000376 | the Wick contractions that produce (21); after taking matrix elements between
000377 | finite-energy vectors, both sides have the same finite mode coefficient at
000378 | every m. Combining (20) and (24) proves the fully ordered local route
000379 | 
000380 |   epsilon->0 after sigma->0 after N->infinity.             (25)
