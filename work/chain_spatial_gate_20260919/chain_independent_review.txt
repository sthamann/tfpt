1. Two-stage Feshbach audit, including excited matter
-----------------------------------------------------

Write H=mu V+A, where A=kappa sum L_i+J sum HE_i.  Let P be the kernel of V:
all source labels are aligned, while the matter state is arbitrary.  Let P0
be the 240-dimensional bottom eigenspace of PAP and P1=P-P0.

For fixed m, PAP has bottom value 3 kappa m/4, one matter vector
psi_alpha^(m+1) for each aligned root alpha, and a positive separation from
P1.  Positivity of the HE_i terms cannot add a bottom vector.  This part of
the proof and the edge-spectrum replay are sound.

The first Feshbach map onto P has an expansion in epsilon=1/mu.  Its
irreducible coefficient K_r at epsilon^r contains r+1 insertions of A and has
only Q=1-P intermediate states.  HE and all source-diagonal pieces preserve
every source label.  One L_i insertion can change only source i.

Therefore an aligned-source matrix element alpha->beta with beta!=alpha
requires at least m L insertions, independently of the intermediate matter
state.  In particular:

  - K_r is diagonal in the aligned root label for r<m-1;
  - at r=m-1, an offdiagonal term contains exactly m L insertions, one at
    every source site;
  - after any proper subset of those m insertions the source configuration is
    not aligned, so the minimal path cannot pass through P1 or any other
    aligned-source matter block before its final endpoint.

Now perform the second reduction from P to P0.  Lower-order P0-P1 couplings
may exist, but every such lower-order segment is diagonal in the aligned root
label.  Products of them through P1 can change the common scalar energy, but
remain diagonal in alpha; native transitivity makes the final P0 diagonal
entry independent of alpha.  To create an offdiagonal folded contribution,
at least one irreducible segment must itself change alpha to beta.  That
segment already costs r>=m-1.  At total order m-1 there is no remaining order
for an additional P1 segment.  Hence the displayed minimal path is the
complete nonscalar coefficient at epsilon^(m-1).

This event-count argument explicitly answers the review question: scalar
lower-order terms coupled to aligned-source excited matter cannot produce an
earlier nonscalar alpha hopping, and they do not modify the leading
nonscalar coefficient.

2. Ordered path integral
------------------------

The identity

  sum_sigma prod_(k=1..m-1) 1/b(S_k) = 2^(m-1)

is correct for the open path.

With t_1=0, the path-edge differences x_i=t_(i+1)-t_i give a triangular
unit-determinant map from R^(m-1) to R^(m-1).  Thus the full integral factors
into m-1 copies of int_R exp(-|x|)dx=2.

The regions defined by the m! possible orderings of t_1,...,t_m partition the
anchored space up to measure-zero boundaries.  In the chamber whose first k
vertices form S_k, the coefficient of the kth positive time gap is exactly
the number b(S_k) of path edges crossing that cut.  Integration over the
positive gaps gives prod_k 1/b(S_k).  Summing the chambers therefore gives
2^(m-1).  Anchoring t_1=0 removes the translation zero mode and introduces no
missing volume factor.

3. Matter factor and native transition classes
-----------------------------------------------

At leading order all m source changes use the unique reflection r sending
alpha to the chosen nonfixed beta.  The two end registers are acted on once
and end in psi_beta.  Each of the m-1 internal registers is acted on twice;
r^2=I leaves psi_alpha, whose overlap with the final psi_beta is

  t=<psi_beta,psi_alpha>.

The matter factor is therefore exactly t^(m-1), independent of event order.
The lock denominator for a proper subset S is mu(1-t)b(S).  Combining this
with the ordered path identity gives the stated coefficients:

  t=1/2 : a_m,
  t=0   : 0,
  t=-1  : (-1/2)^(m-1) a_m.

The Feshbach and L-event signs multiply to minus for every m.  The m=2 result
reduces to -(kappa^2/mu)(2A32-F)/3600, agreeing with the pinned independent
m=2 certificate.

4. Fixed-m uniqueness and its exact boundary
---------------------------------------------

For fixed finite m, the P0/P1 matter separation is positive, the off-lock
separation grows with mu, and the finite-dimensional Feshbach map is analytic
near epsilon=0.  The leading nonscalar operator has a simple extremal level;
its next level is separated by

  a_m [16+2(-1/2)^(m-1)] > 0.

The omitted terms are smaller by one power of 1/mu for fixed m.  Standard
finite-dimensional analytic perturbation therefore supplies some finite
mu_0(m,kappa,J) above which the full ground state is simple.

This is only an existence statement.  The proof provides neither an explicit
remainder constant nor a usable or m-uniform mu_0.  Since a_m itself decreases
rapidly with m, no thermodynamic gap, propagating mode or spatial continuum
follows.
