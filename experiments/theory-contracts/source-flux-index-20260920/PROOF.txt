UR.SOURCE.FLUX_INDEX.01 -- conditional charge selection and flux-index bridge
20 September 2026

QUESTION AND SCOPE

Does the already fixed ten-channel E8 boundary dictionary select its
candidate gauge charge from the original conserved markings, and does the
index of an elementary gauge-flux event then select the existing n vertex?

Success means an exact charge classification, the signed unit-flux index,
and the integral neutral-lattice quotient with the original charge markings.
It does not mean existence of this gauge field in the original source.
No spectrum optimization or new lattice Hamiltonian is undertaken here.
The first added physical premise, when the index is interpreted physically,
is a compact U(1) connection coupled to these ten chiral channels through g.
The native two-species rotor has not been identified with that premise.

INPUTS

The existing conditional boundary has Gamma=Z^10 with bilinear form
K=diag(1^9,-1), original global charge q=(1,...,1), and
Y=(-1/3,-1/3,-1/3,1/2,1/2,0,0,0,1,1).

Let a=(1,1,1,-1,-1,-1,-1,-1). The already declared E8 embedding is

  F(r)=(r-(a.r)a/2, 0, -a.r),    r in the standard E8 lattice.

This F, including its local lattice and energy provenance, is an input.
In particular, using F does not provide an independent origin of E8.
The previous contract named n=(a,-1,3). Below we recover this vector from
charge conservation and a flux index instead of imposing n-neutrality as
an additional selection condition.

1. CHARGE SELECTION WITHOUT IMPOSING THE DESIRED VERTEX

Write an integral charge covector g=(b,u,t). Full E8 neutrality is
g.F(r)=0 for every root, hence for all r in R^8 because the roots span R^8:

  b - (a.b)a/2 - t a = 0.

The component of b perpendicular to a therefore vanishes. Since each a_i
equals +/-1 and b is integral, b=v a for an integer v. With a.a=8 the
remaining equation is t=-3v. Thus every such charge is exactly

  g(v,u)=(v a,u,-3v),       u,v in Z.

For this full class, the pure and mixed two-dimensional anomaly bilinears
are

  A_gg = g^T K^{-1}g = u^2-v^2,
  A_qg = q^T K^{-1}g = u+v,
  A_Yg = Y^T K^{-1}g = u+v.

Here K^{-1}=K. The mixed equations can equally be read as the original
markings of the signed index vector in a unit gauge-flux background.
Preserving the original q OR Y marking under such flux therefore requires
u=-v. It also makes A_gg vanish. A nonzero primitive integral covector
then has v=+/-1, giving

  g=+/- (a,-1,-3)=+/- K n.

Pure gauge-anomaly cancellation alone would also allow u=+v. That branch
has A_qg=A_Yg=2v and cannot retain these original markings for nonzero v.
Thus the old n-neutrality requirement has been replaced by an independently
stated conservation requirement on the existing source dictionary.
The fixed F embedding remains an assumption throughout this argument.

These bilinear identities are not a construction of a regulator or a full
anomaly-free theory of gravity. The chiral gravitational coefficient is
still signature(K)=8.

2. THE ELEMENTARY FLUX INDEX IS THE EXISTING n

Assume, conditionally, a smooth compact U(1) connection A on a fixed closed
oriented spin two-manifold, with integer flux nu=(1/2pi) integral F_A.
For channel i, with chirality s_i=K_ii and gauge charge g_i, the signed
Dirac index is

  I_i = s_i g_i nu.

Changing orientation or the convention for conjugate insertions reverses
the whole index. With our chosen orientation and g=K n,

  I(nu)=nu K g=nu n.

An elementary holonomy spectral-flow check gives the same result: for the
chiral levels E_i,m(a)=s_i(m+delta_i-g_i a), changing a from 0 to 1 gives
signed downward crossings s_i g_i. Any fixed delta_i in (0,1) keeps the
endpoints away from zero. The count is independent of this twist.
This auxiliary spectral flow is not an identification with a native Clock.

Zero-mode saturation in the one-flux sector therefore has net fermion
content n, up to conjugation. Extra zero-mode pairs, if present in a special
background, do not change the index. On the sphere the twisted spin bundles
O(g_i nu-1) give exactly the minimal absolute counts by Riemann--Roch.
This is a conditional continuum background calculation, not a new choice
of the physical spatial geometry of TFPT.

For nu=1 a lowest-degree local representative has the form

  O_n ~ psi_1 psi_2 psi_3
        psi_4^dagger ... psi_9^dagger
        psi_10 (dbar psi_10) (dbar^2 psi_10).

Klein factors, ordering and the overall normalization must be supplied in
a full operator definition. The three identical same-chirality fermions
cannot be put at precisely the same point without point splitting. Taylor
expansion and antisymmetry produce derivative orders 0,1,2. Consequently

  minimal elementary fermion count = 9+3 = 12,
  minimal derivative order = 0+1+2 = 3,
  (h,hbar)_free = (9/2,9/2),    Delta_free=9,    spin=0.

The gauge, q and Y charges vanish, and the parity is even. These reproduce
the previously computed n-vertex data. They do not claim new discovery of
the zero-mode/index mechanism, which is standard chiral gauge theory.

3. WHAT THE INDEX DOES AND DOES NOT GENERATE

The index identifies the fermionic selection rule of a flux-sector
correlator. In the language of gauge theory it identifies the possible
't Hooft insertion. It does NOT by itself prove a nonzero coefficient for
an added local Hamiltonian term lambda cos(n.phi+theta), a dilute instanton
gas, a chosen theta angle, a vacuum, or an RG flow to the previously chosen
E8 energy matrix. Delta_free=9 is in particular not a relevant perturbation
of the original free 1+1d point.

A fixed-background Grassmann calculation can be Gaussian while carrying
zero modes. Saturating those modes and summing dynamical gauge/flux sectors
is additional data. It is not the ordinary invertible Gaussian elimination
which the earlier source audit excluded as a generator of interactions.

Narayanan--Neuberger hep-lat/9609031, pp.2--5, supplies a concrete primary
comparison: a chiral anomaly-free 11112 model has a derivative-dressed
't Hooft insertion; they explicitly distinguish its correlator from an
unjustified dilute-gas exponentiation. We use that methodological distinction,
not its particular spectrum or numerical amplitude for our 9+1 content.

4. EXACT INTEGRAL QUOTIENT AND INHERITED MARKINGS

Use the already known integral unimodular decomposition

  Gamma = F(E8) + Z e_R + Z m,
  e_R=-e_9,       m=(a,0,3),       e_R+m=n,

with pair form diag(1,-1) and orthogonality to F(E8). For
x=F(r)+A e_R+B m, the gauge charge is g.x=A-B. Hence

  ker(g dot)=F(E8) + Z n,
  ker(g dot)/Z n is isometric to E8.

This is a statement about the entire integral lattice, not just its roots.
The quotient form is well defined since n is null and orthogonal to every
g-neutral vector. Both q and Y descend to the quotient because q.n=Y.n=0.

All g_i are odd. For every integral x, g.x equals x^T K x modulo two.
Thus g-neutral local vertices are even, and the quotient is a bosonic
lattice. The earlier odd c fields have charges +/-1, so this quotient does
not retain them as neutral local matter fields. Charged sectors or dressed
fields require an additional physical treatment; no universal exclusion of
charged fermions is asserted.

Identifying representatives x and x+n dynamically is NOT automatic from
this quotient calculation. Their original free dimensions and correlations
can differ. A full gauged state and correlation-function reduction must
justify any physical use of this identification.

5. A SECOND, EXPLICIT A8 PRESENTATION OF THE SAME QUOTIENT

For bookkeeping only, flip the first nine coordinates by their gauge-charge
signs. The gauge covector becomes (1^9,-3) and n becomes n'=(1^9,3).
The source charges q,Y must be transformed with the same sign matrix;
this is not a new physical particle-hole symmetry claim.

A neutral integral vector (y,k) obeys sum(y)=3k. Define

  pi(y,k)=y-(k/3)1_9.

Then sum(pi)=0 and |pi|^2=|y|^2-k^2, the original indefinite norm.
Its integral kernel is Z n'. Its image contains the A8 root lattice with
index three: k mod3 labels the three cosets, and k=1 has representatives
with three entries 1 and six entries 0 before projection. Since det(A8)=9,
the image has determinant 9/3^2=1. It is even because
|y|^2-k^2 = sum(y)-k = 2k modulo two. Thus it is the positive even
unimodular rank-eight lattice E8.

Every root class can be represented with k=-1,0,1 by adding n'.
Norm two then forces:
  k=0: y=e_i-e_j, i!=j, giving 72 roots;
  k=1: three y_i=1, all others0, giving C(9,3)=84 roots;
  k=-1: their negatives, giving another84 roots.

The first representatives are fermion bilinears; the latter have four
fermion factors, three in the nine-channel chirality and one in the opposite
chirality. Their original free weights are (3/2,1/2), not (1,0).
The quotient norm-two property is therefore not itself a derivation of
dimension-one chiral currents over the original free vacuum. No such IR
step is smuggled into the root count 72+84+84=240.

6. RETURN TO THE ORIGINAL SOURCE

P1 states unit winding for a primitive boundary kernel. That is not already
an operator map identifying it with the compact U(1) flux nu above, with
the ten matter charges g, or with a sum over dynamical gauge connections.
The original two-species equal-charge rotor also has no supplied map to
this ten-channel chiral source.

The current source-operator-origin contract sharpens the other missing
piece: under its fixed common T dictionary, the unchanged 64-CAR/60-CCR
Fock algebra lacks the diagonal-center sign carried by the required odd
local boundary fields. The pre-compression Clifford transitions are not
already implemented extra source fields. Our flux-index calculation does
not supply them or evade this theorem.

The concrete native target is therefore a charge-, grading-, and time-faithful
realization of g and its flux-changing sectors from the original source.
Only then would the conditional index above determine an actual source
insertion. Its amplitude, phase, vacuum and common infrared dynamics would
still have to be evaluated. The conditional algebra provides no physical
3+1d limit, chirality/mirror theorem, gravitational sector or complete TOE.
