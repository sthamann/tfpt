1. The first-moment sum rule is exact

For a self-adjoint observable O and a normalized ground vector g, define
S_O by the positive excitation spectral measure of O g. Then

 M1(O)=<g|O(H-E0)O|g>=1/2 <g|[O,[H,O]]|g>.

Source coordinates x_e commute with the conditional matter energy and
the diagonal distance energy. A primitive L_e changes only source e.
For distinct sources e,f this gives the operator identity

 [x_e,[H,x_f]]=0.

Expanding O_+=(x_1+x_2)/sqrt2 and O_-=(x_1-x_2)/sqrt2 gives

 M1(O_+)=M1(O_-)=1/2 [M1(x_1)+M1(x_2)].

This argument needs no large-coupling limit and no unique ground state.
It applies to each real coordinate, and to their summed response, provided
the same normalization is used. In a degenerate ground space it applies
to any chosen ground vector or mixture. A zero-frequency elastic weight
does not affect M1.

The printed rounded table is arithmetically consistent with the claimed
scale separation: first-line ratio 122.98715658, mean-energy ratio
142.45136026. Multiplying the printed means and weights gives
0.00251678285594 and 0.00251670870318. Their small discrepancy is compatible
with the table's rounding; it neither contradicts nor verifies the quoted
full-precision equality 0.00251677823934. The 99.85 percent line weight,
the full spectrum and chi_21(1) have not been replayed here.

2. An additional short-time consequence is available

If the ground state or ground density matrix is invariant under swapping
the two sources together with the corresponding matter endpoints, O_+
and O_- have opposite parity and their cross spectral measures vanish.
With the user's convention chi_21(t)=-i theta(t)<[x_2(t),x_1]>,

 chi_21(t) = -theta(t) integral sin(omega t) [S_+(domega)-S_-(domega)].

The exact equal-first-moment identity cancels its linear coefficient.
On this finite Hilbert space the Taylor expansion is justified and

 chi_21(t) = theta(t) t^3/6 [M3(O_+)-M3(O_-)] + O(t^5).

Thus the new response code has a sharper test than agreement at t=1:
the first derivative at zero must vanish, and the leading allowed
cross-response is cubic. This is conditional on the stated reflection
invariance; it is not imposed on a chosen symmetry-breaking ground vector.
It follows algebraically from the same H and observables, not from fitting
the supplied numerical chi value. No value of the third moment is claimed.

3. The mu-independent locality estimate has the stated mechanism

Write H=H_D+K, H_D=mu sum V_(e,e+1), K=J sum H_E,e+kappa sum L_e.
The terms in H_D commute. For an observable with support X, its conjugation
by exp(i t H_D) only involves terms meeting X; all other terms can be moved
past both the meeting terms and the observable and canceled. Its support
therefore enlarges by a fixed neighbor collar, not successively along the
whole chain. Its norm is unchanged.

The same reasoning applies to every term of K in the interaction picture.
On this bounded-degree chain the new support sizes and overlap counts are
fixed, while the individual norms remain bounded by constants times
J+kappa. A time-dependent finite-range Lieb-Robinson bound then has
velocity bounded by C(J+kappa), independent of mu and length. Returning
to the original observables adds a fixed support offset. The bound does
not give a light speed, an observed dispersion, or existence of a dynamics
limit when mu itself is made divergent with size.

This is an instance of an established theorem, rather than a new general
locality theorem: Lemm and Wessel, Enhanced Lieb-Robinson bounds for
commuting long-range interactions, Theorem 15 and Section 5.3:
https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19241v4

4. Exact soluble sector and the lower-energy witness

For the pure source permutations P_l, the uniform vector s satisfies
P_l s=s. At J=mu=0 this makes s^tensor m tensor H_matter invariant.
The native second moment gives

 average_l r_l tensor r_l=(I+Swap)/5,
 H_s=(kappa/5) sum_edges (4I-Swap).

For a single altered matter basis value, I-Swap moves the location of
that value by the graph Laplacian. The dispersion on the infinite chain
is therefore 2kappa(1-cos q)/5, with quadratic small-q behavior and maximum
group speed 2kappa/5 in the specified lattice units.

The lower-energy eigenvector is also constructible directly. Define
orthonormal source vectors

 c_j=(1/sqrt60) sum_alpha (psi_alpha)_j |alpha>, j=1,...,4.

Since P_l acts by a pure permutation and psi_(r_l alpha)=r_l psi_alpha,
it acts on this basis as transpose(r_l)=conjugate(r_l). Consequently

 eta=(1/2) sum_j c_j tensor |j>

is invariant under P_l tensor r_l on source e and its left matter
register. Pair each source with its left matter register and leave the
rightmost register arbitrary. On this vector each primitive edge action
leaves its pair invariant and acts by r_l on the right register. The
average is I/2, even if that right register belongs to the next entangled
pair. Thus every L_e has eigenvalue 1/2 on the same full vector and

 E_comparison=kappa m/2 < 3kappa m/5=E_F.

This proves that the ferromagnetic reference of the soluble sector is
not a full-model ground state, without claiming that the comparison
eigenvector is the true ground state. The paired c_j construction also
shows exactly where pure source permutations enter this conclusion.
