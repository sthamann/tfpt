Verdict
-------
The affine CAR-current formulas are exact after taking the inductive limit on
the finite-particle/hole core.  They are not identities on the whole Fock
space of a fixed finite window: explicit edge operators remain.  The pair
formula is valid on that core only for a != b.  For a=b the pair operator is
identically zero, so the displayed nonzero right-hand side is false.

The sine dispersion admits the claimed common-current frequency estimate for
M=floor(L^(1/3)).  The same Hamiltonians converge to the linear generator on
each fixed finite-excitation core.  These are conditional facts about the
specified free CAR model.  They neither derive eight identical copies nor
construct the missing native transition TRANS.SEAMCAR.E8CURRENT.01.

Conventions
-----------
W_M={-M+1/2,...,M-1/2}.  The sea vacuum Omega has every r<0 occupied and every
r>0 empty.  Let p(r)=1 for r<0 and 0 otherwise, and use

  :c^*_(a,r)c_(b,s): = c^*_(a,r)c_(b,s)-delta_ab delta_rs p(r) I.

For integer n,

  E_n^(ab)(M) = sum_(r,r+n in W_M) :c^*_(a,r)c_(b,r+n):,
  A_n^(ab)(M) = sum_(r,n-r in W_M) c_(a,r)c_(b,n-r).

1. Adjoint and vacuum response
------------------------------
Changing variables s=r+n in the finite sum gives exactly

  (E_n^(ab)(M))^* = E_(-n)^(ba)(M).

For n>0, E_n Omega=0.  In E_(-n) Omega only the n half-integer modes
0<r<n cross the Fermi surface.  The resulting particle-hole states are
orthonormal.  Therefore, once M>n,

  ||E_(-n)^(ab) Omega||^2 = n.

The checker verifies this with exact integer amplitudes for n=1,2,3.

2. Current commutator and the finite-window edge term
------------------------------------------------------
The elementary CAR identity is

 [c^*_(a,r)c_(b,r+m), c^*_(c,s)c_(d,s+n)]
 = delta_bc delta_(r+m,s) c^*_(a,r)c_(d,s+n)
   -delta_ad delta_(s+n,r) c^*_(c,s)c_(b,r+m).

In an infinite sum, normal ordering then yields

 [E_m^(ab),E_n^(cd)]
 = delta_bc E_(m+n)^(ad)-delta_ad E_(m+n)^(cb)
   +m delta_(m+n,0) delta_ad delta_bc I.                 (1)

The scalar is not inserted by hand.  If m+n=0, it is

  sum_r [p(r)-p(r+m)] = m.                              (2)

In a finite window the first surviving sum requires all of
r, r+m, r+m+n to lie in W_M.  The first E on the right of (1) requires only
r and r+m+n.  The second sum likewise requires s, s+n, s+n+m, whereas its E
requires only the two endpoints.  Hence the exact finite formula is (1) plus
an edge operator B_(m,n;M), supported where the omitted intermediate mode has
left W_M.  B is generally nonzero on the full finite Fock space.

Let D_R be the span of states whose occupation differs from the sea only for
|r|<=R.  A sufficient cutoff is

  M-1/2 > R+|m|+|n|.                                    (3)

Every monomial in B then lies beyond the excitation support and has both
endpoints on the same side of the Fermi surface.  Its normal-ordered action on
D_R is zero.  Equations (1)-(2) therefore stabilize on D_R.

The exact checker tests the genuinely off-diagonal central bracket

 [E_1^(01),E_(-1)^(10)] = E_0^(00)-E_0^(11)+I

and the noncentral bracket with m=n=1 and output mode 2 on all 16 basis states
of D_(1/2) at M=4.  It also exhausts all 256 states at M=2 and finds an
explicit state where the first finite-window identity fails.

3. Pair bracket and the a=b correction
---------------------------------------
For a != b, direct CAR reduction has no exchange-flavor contraction and gives

 [A_n^(ab)(M),A_n^(ab)(M)^*]
 = sum_(r in S_(n,M)) [I-N_(a,r)-N_(b,n-r)],             (4)

where S_(n,M)={r in W_M : n-r in W_M}.  After normal ordering, the scalar in
(4) is

 sum_(r in S) [1-p(r)-p(n-r)] = n                       (5)

for M>|n|.  Thus the exact finite formula is

 [A_n^(ab)(M),A_n^(ab)(M)^*]
 = nI-E_0^(aa)(M)-E_0^(bb)(M)
   + sum_(r in W_M\S) (:N_(a,r):+:N_(b,r):).             (6)

The last sum is the finite-window boundary charge.  It vanishes on D_R if

  M-1/2 > R+|n|.                                        (7)

This proves the stated pair identity on the stabilized core for a != b, and
also shows why it is not a global finite-window identity.

For a=b, pair the summand labelled r with the one labelled n-r:

  c_(a,r)c_(a,n-r)+c_(a,n-r)c_(a,r)=0.

The fixed-point term, if present, is c_(a,r)^2=0.  Consequently

  A_n^(aa)(M)=0                                         (8)

for every M and n.  The proposed right-hand side is not zero; on Omega it is
n Omega.  The condition a != b is therefore essential, not cosmetic.

4. Common time estimate
-----------------------
Let

  H_(L,M)=sum_(a,r in W_M) e_L(r):N_(a,r):,
  e_L(r)=L/(2*pi) sin(2*pi*r/L).

For r,r+n in W_M,

 |e_L(r)-e_L(r+n)+n|
 = |integral_r^(r+n) [1-cos(2*pi*s/L)] ds|
 <= 2*pi^2 |n| M^2/L^2,                                (9)

using 1-cos x<=x^2/2 and |s|<M.  There are at most 2M bilinears and each has
operator norm at most one, so the triangle inequality gives

 ||[H_(L,M),E_n^(ab)(M)]+n E_n^(ab)(M)||
 <= 4*pi^2 |n| M^3/L^2.                                (10)

With M=floor(L^(1/3)), M^3<=L, hence (10) is bounded by

  4*pi^2 |n|/L -> 0.                                   (11)

This is a full finite-Fock operator-norm bound, so it is stronger than merely
checking matrix elements on D_R.

There is also a direct common-generator statement.  Put

  H_0=sum_(a,r) r:N_(a,r):.

Since |sin x-x|<=|x|^3/6,

  |e_L(r)-r| <= (2*pi^2/3)|r|^3/L^2.                   (12)

On the subspace D_(R,q) with at most q particle/hole deviations supported in
|r|<=R, normal ordering cancels the sea contribution and

 ||(H_(L,M)-H_0)|D_(R,q)||
 <= (2*pi^2/3) q R^3/L^2.                              (13)

For fixed R,q, any M>R contains the whole core, and (13) tends to zero.  Thus
the same limiting generator produces the frequencies in (11); no separate
mode-dependent clock is needed inside this conditional model.
