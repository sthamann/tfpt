1. The covariance correction is necessary and sufficient
============================================================

The original statement that all conditional cross covariances vanish is
false for a boundary/bridge pattern. There, the two remaining local terms
touch the B and C legs of the same Omega packet. Its reduced state is

  rho_BC=P_sym/10=(I+Swap)/20,

so, with tr4 the normalized trace,

  Tr[rho_BC (X tensor Y)]-tr4(X)tr4(Y)
    =(1/5) tr4[(X-tr4(X))(Y-tr4(Y))].

This is generally nonzero. A direct reduced native contraction for the
1232 geometry also gives nonzero cross operators, independently confirming
that the old factorization could not be retained.

The corrected proof uses exactly this identity. The external coefficients
of X and Y act on disjoint remaining source/matter factors. Positivity of
the 2-by-2 operator covariance matrix gives operator Cauchy-Schwarz,

  ||Cov(X,Y)|| <= sqrt(v1 v2)/5.

Adding the two ordered cross terms therefore gives

  b^2 <= v1+v2+(2/5)sqrt(v1 v2).

For a bridge first traced over its leg in the other packet, Kadison's
inequality gives Var(E_leg L)<=E_leg Var(L), so its remaining-leg variance
is bounded by the certified two-matter variance. This justifies using
11/320 for a phase1 or phase3 bridge and 29/1200 for a nontrivial phase0
bridge. For the uniform phase0 source, the partially traced bridge is
exactly 3I/4, and only in this special case does the connected covariance
vanish.

The central geometry is different: the two local terms trace one leg from
each of two independent outer packets. Their conditional product factorizes
in its actual operator order even when both terms retain the middle matter
register. Thus the zero-covariance sums 23/300 and 4/75 used for 2132/2312
and 2112 are valid.

All boundary comparisons now use the corrected bound. In particular,

  corr(2/75,11/320)=293/4800+sqrt(330)/1500,
  corr(3/80,11/320)=23/320+sqrt(33)/400,

and corr(2/75,29/1200)<=77/1200. The updated scalar lower bounds still
exceed U=391/320. The smallest of the listed comparison floors is the

================================
