Focused review: joint D4 residue/compiler dictionary
Date: 2026-09-21
Scope: read-only. No repository, ledger, paper, or graph changes.

VERDICT

The proposed X is a genuine constructive closure of the finite module-
identification gap left by v146. It preserves the original later-paper D4:
Sigma remains a reflection, not a half-turn. Unlike v69's simplified B, this
construction transports the actual compiler Q and therefore its actual Q+,
Q-, products, and relations simultaneously.

It does not by itself derive the dictionary from primitive P1. The remaining
source question is now sharply separated from the finite algebra: why the
primitive seam selects this integral module embedding (and its normalization).

1. Exact joint transport

Let H have columns

  h1=(1,0,0,-1), h2=(0,1,0,-1), h3=(0,0,1,-1),

so the canonical residue Gram is

  F=H^T H=[[2,1,1],[1,2,1],[1,1,2]], det F=4.

For the square rotation and reflection in the question, their induced residue
matrices are

  Rh=[[-1,-1,-1],[1,0,0],[0,1,0]],
  Sh=[[1,0,0],[-1,-1,-1],[0,0,1]].

For every

  X(a,b)=[[-a-2b,-a-4b,b],
          [ a+2b,-a,   -b],
          [ a+2b, a,    b]],

one has exactly

  Rh X = X G,
  Sh X = X T_A,
  det X = 4 b (a+2b)^2.

Consequently

  X Sigma X^-1 = Sh Rh,

because Sigma=T_A G. Thus both geometric reflection classes are preserved:
T_A maps to Sh, while Sigma maps to the adjacent reflection Sh Rh. This is
the original D4 of v97/v98/v141/v146.

At a=-1,b=1,

  X=[[-1,-3,1],[1,1,-1],[1,-1,1]], det X=4,

and direct calculation gives

  X Q X^-1 = [[2,-3,-4],[0,2,1],[-1,0,2]],
  X^T F X = [[4,0,0],[0,20,-8],[0,-8,4]].

Since Qh is obtained by a single similarity and Sigma_h by the same X, both
halves are transported jointly:

  Qh,+ = X Q+ X^-1
       = (Qh + Sigma_h Qh Sigma_h)/2,
  Qh,- = X Q- X^-1
       = (Qh - Sigma_h Qh Sigma_h)/2.

All noncommutative words and projector incidences are therefore preserved.
The earlier projector obstruction against v69's simplified B is irrelevant:
this construction does not replace the actual Q- by that B.

2. What v97/v98/v141/v146 already contained

v97 already constructed T_A uniquely from the Q+ eigenlines plus the two
anchor conditions T.1=1 and T.a=a, proved <T_A,Sigma>=D4, and solved the
cyclic intertwiner equation to puncture homology. It found rational
conjugacy, no GL(3,Z) conjugacy, and minimum cyclic-intertwiner determinant 2
(verification/v97_sheet_conjugation_bridge.py:14-53,99-184). It did not impose
the puncture reflection simultaneously.

v98 established the exact internal integer D4 dictionary: G=T_A Sigma has
the B1 line plus E plane, T_A and Sigma are the two reflection classes, and
the Q+ grading aligns with the conjugacy classes. Its status still leaves the
parabolic-geometric realization of the documented Q as GATE.QGEO
(verification/v98_discriminant_dictionary.py:1-46,70-123).

v141 selected the sheet-twisted cyclic character assignment (2,3,1) and
constructed a Q+=1-line-preserving complex equivalence to -U. It explicitly
leaves the premise that the integer D4 model is the parabolic geometry
(verification/v141_deck_selection.py:1-56,78-147). It is a character-basis
selection, not the real residue-lattice X above.

v146 matched the Mobius actions class by class: delta on omega_k, inversion
iota, T_A as the iota class, and Sigma as the delta-iota class. Its own
recorded [P] residue is exactly the module identification "generation space
= H1 with parabolic weights" (verification/v146_moebius_d4.py:1-40,
70-115). It supplies the group-action target that X now meets, but no explicit
joint real/integral X and no transport of Q.

Therefore the new contribution is precise: it strengthens the previous
separate class/character match into one joint lattice intertwiner and carries
the full compiler operator through it.

3. Are a,b already selected?

D4 equivariance alone leaves two Schur scales, one on B1 and one on E. This
is exactly the two-parameter X(a,b); invertibility only requires
b(a+2b) != 0. So v97/v98/v141/v146 do not by themselves select a=-1,b=1.

The original corpus nevertheless supplies two strong normalization facts:

  - the residue lattice and its pairing are canonical, integral A3 data with
    det F=4 (tfpt_1_architecture_e8.tex:1928-1959);
  - v97 treats the least nonzero determinant of the cyclic lattice
    intertwiner as the sheet index, rather than ignoring it
    (verification/v97_sheet_conjugation_bridge.py:40-46,162-181).

If the same primitive/minimal-index rule is applied to the FULL D4
intertwiner, then

  min |det X(a,b)| = 4

for integral a,b. The minimizers are

  (a,b)=(-1,1),(-3,1),(1,-1),(3,-1).

Overall sign identifies the first/last and second/third. The remaining two
are related by the already-owned central rotation:

  X(-3,1)=X(-1,1) G^2.

They induce exactly the same Gram. Thus minimal integral index selects ONE
D4 orbit, and a=-1,b=1 is a valid canonical representative, not a fitted
continuous choice.

Equivalently, after removing the common factor 4, the induced form

  M0=(1/4)X^T F X=[[1,0,0],[0,5,-2],[0,-2,1]]

is the unique positive-definite integral unimodular D4-invariant Gram. The
general invariant symmetric form is

  M(u,v)=[[u-4v,0,0],[0,u,-2v],[0,-2v,v]],
  det M=v(u-4v)^2;

positive integral unimodularity forces v=1,u=5. This is a real uniqueness
result available to the construction.

Honest typing: the sources praise/use minimum lattice index, but none of the
four bridge scripts explicitly declares "choose the minimum full-D4 index"
as a primitive physical selection rule. Therefore the continuous a,b freedom
is algebraically removed once the existing minimal-index convention is
adopted; what remains physical is whether P1 selects that convention. It
should not be reported merely as an unexplored two-parameter freedom.

No already-established compiler Gram directly fixes a,b instead. The later
cover program's v601 form J is a Hermitian Lorentz-signature (1,2) form on
the mu3 cover and realizes the transposed/dual parabolic; it explicitly leaves
the Weil-twisted RP comparison and seam-line identification open
(verification/v601_equivariant_dual.py:2-62). It is not the positive residue
Gram F transported here. v600 realizes the actual compiler pair on that
cover but states canonicity open (verification/v600_joint_embedding.py:2-58).

4. Does P1 specify a unique raw kernel function?

No. The original P1 statement specifies one oriented seam, reflection
positivity, unit winding, and the scalar normalization c3=1/(8pi)
(tfpt_1_architecture_e8.tex:163-176). It supplies neither an argument space
nor a formula C_Sigma(x,y), and later calls its analytic Calderon/winding
premises imported hypotheses (tfpt_2_standard_model.tex:2413-2419).

The papers do contain more explicit kernel constructions, but none is a
unique raw P1 function:

  - M=1+iA with A^2=-1 and P=M/2 is a finite quasifree/Calderon
    polarization template; the paper explicitly keeps the premise merge
    conditional (tfpt_1_architecture_e8.tex:1615-1636).
  - K(x,y)=omega(theta(x)y) is an RP form on later integral orders and
    depends on a declared state; those tested kernels are even reported
    non-PSD in that application (tfpt_2_standard_model.tex:849-865).
  - C_g=N_g^-1 and the metric-coupled K_g are explicit operator families,
    but depend on g, its Laplace/Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator, projections,
    h and T_grav (tfpt_2_standard_model.tex:3634-3666). They prove a
    conditional RP/gap statement over a Calderon ball, not selection of one
    primitive C_Sigma.

So the correct statement is not "the paper has no kernel formulas". It has
conditional templates and families. The precise missing primitive datum is a
unique selected raw two-point/Calderon operator and its restriction to the
residue lattice. That source-side restriction is what could justify the
minimal-index X orbit and its Gram physically.
