# Local coisotropic constraints for the actual reduced Hamiltonian

Date: 2026-09-06. Bounded Round13, non-RH. Locally integrated unpromoted research.

## 1. Exact local result, with its quantifiers

Let (M,omega) be a finite-dimensional smooth symplectic phase space and
H_g a jointly smooth family of autonomous Hamiltonians for g near zero.
Use X_H f={f,H}. Suppose that on a neighborhood of z_*:

    J_0(z_*)=0,  rank dJ_0(z_*)=r,
    {J_0,i,J_0,j}=0,  {J_0,i,H_0}=0,  dH_0(z_*) != 0.    (1)

No independence of dH_0 from the r constraint differentials is required
for the following theorem, although the actual point below has that
stronger property as well.

**Theorem.** After shrinking to a neighborhood of z_* and a coupling
interval |g|<delta, there are smooth local symplectomorphisms Phi_g with

    Phi_0=id,  Phi_g(z_*)=z_*,
    H_g composed with Phi_g = H_0+c(g),
    c(g)=H_g(z_*)-H_0(z_*).                              (2)

Consequently, on a common smaller neighborhood the functions

    K_i(g)=J_0,i composed with Phi_g^-1                  (3)

obey exactly, not just to finite perturbative order,

    K(g)=J_0+O(g),  K(g,z_*)=0,
    {K_i,K_j}=0,  {K_i,H_g}=0,  rank dK=r.               (4)

The surface C_g={K(g)=0} is a regular codimension-r coisotropic surface
through the same point z_*, invariant under the actual H_g flow for as
long as that flow remains inside the chosen neighborhood. Its local
characteristic distribution has rank r. This is an existence statement
on a finite phase-space patch, not on an entire recurrent orbit or torus.

### Constructive proof by characteristics and a coupling-parameter flow

Choose a hypersurface Sigma through z_* transverse to X_H0. It remains
transverse to X_Hg for sufficiently small |g|. The ordinary local flowbox
theorem with parameters supplies coordinates

    z = F_g^t(xi),   xi in Sigma, |t| small,

where F_g is the physical-time flow of the ACTUAL H_g. In particular no
symplectic property of Sigma is presumed. Define, on this flowbox,

    S_g(F_g^t(xi))
       = integral_0^t [partial_g H_g(F_g^s(xi))
                       -partial_g H_g(z_*)] ds.         (5)

ODE smooth dependence and the implicit function theorem make S_g jointly
smooth in g and z. It solves

    {S_g,H_g}=partial_g H_g-partial_g H_g(z_*),
    S_g|_Sigma=0.                                       (6)

At z_*, tangential derivatives along Sigma vanish by the boundary condition.
The derivative in the transverse X_Hg direction also vanishes by (6).
Thus dS_g(z_*)=0. Integrate the nonautonomous COUPLING-parameter equation

    dPhi_g/dg = X_Sg composed with Phi_g,  Phi_0=id.       (7)

This is a local symplectic flow and fixes z_*. Uniform existence for small
|g| follows on a smaller relatively compact patch. Its domain need not
be the full phase space and its vector field need not be complete.
The chain rule gives the exact identity

    d/dg (H_g composed with Phi_g)
      = [partial_g H_g+{H_g,S_g}] composed with Phi_g
      = partial_g H_g(z_*).

Integration proves (2); Poisson-bracket covariance proves (4). Shrinking
once more supplies a common neighborhood contained in all relevant local
images. Smoothness implies the O(g) statement on its compact subsets.

The constant c(g) is part of the comparison identity, not a modification
of H_g. It has no effect on autonomous Hamiltonian vector fields. No
coupling-dependent physical energy subtraction or clock convention has
been justified by this theorem.

Finally T C_g=intersection_i ker dK_i. Nondegeneracy of omega gives
(T C_g)^omega=span{X_Ki}; equation (4) places this span inside T C_g.
That is precisely coisotropy. Hamiltonian invariance follows from
{K_i,H_g}=0. No overdetermined constraint PDE remains to be guessed.

This argument is an explicit parameter-dependent version of local
Hamiltonian straightening. Primary context is Bessa--Lopes Dias,
[Generic dynamics of 4-dimensional Hamiltonian systems, Theorem 4.1](https://hal.iseg.ulisboa.pt/~jldias/preprints/bessadias.pdf),
which states a symplectic flowbox theorem in general dimension. Here the
smooth hypotheses avoid finite-regularity losses; equations (5)--(7)
prove the needed parameter dependence and fixed-point normalization
directly. No global dynamical conclusions from that paper are imported.

## 2. Weak closure and the Jacobi compatibility conditions

The construction already satisfies weak first-class closure with all
structure and propagation coefficients zero. More general representatives
of the SAME local surface are available. If B(g,z) is any smooth invertible
r by r matrix with B(0,z)=I, define J(g)=B(g,z)K(g). Then

    {J_i,H_g}=M_i^j J_j,
    M={B,H_g} B^-1.                                    (8)

Since brackets of the K_i vanish, the brackets {J_i,J_j} belong to the
ideal generated by K, equivalently by J. Hence smooth C_ij^k exist with

    {J_i,J_j}=C_ij^k J_k.                              (9)

These are not arbitrary independently selectable matrices: they come from
actual functions with their Poisson bracket. Jacobi and its compatibility
with evolution therefore hold automatically. For example, restricting
the coefficient identities to the regular surface gives

    cyclic_(i,j,k) [ {J_i,C_jk^l}+C_jk^a C_ia^l ] = 0,

    {C_ij^k,H_g}+C_ij^a M_a^k
       = {J_i,M_j^k}-{J_j,M_i^k}
           +M_j^a C_ia^k-M_i^a C_ja^k.                 (10)

The identities follow by Jacobi followed by differentiation in independent
constraint coordinates at J=0; no off-surface uniqueness of C or M is
asserted. Conversely, a regular surface is weakly invariant precisely
when its defining-function ideal is preserved by X_H. Smooth local
coefficients M then follow from the Hadamard lemma. Coisotropy similarly
requires the defining ideal to be closed under Poisson brackets; ordinary
flow transport of arbitrary section data alone does not ensure that.

Here is a particularly explicit weak, generally non-strong representative.
Let tau_g be the local physical flowbox time, {tau_g,H_g}=1, and put

    u=exp(g tau_g),    J_i=u K_i.

Then

    {J_i,H_g}=g J_i,
    {J_i,J_j}=g u [{K_i,tau_g}J_j-{K_j,tau_g}J_i].      (11)

This preserves the exact same regular coisotropic surface. It exhibits
nonzero allowed propagation/structure coefficients rather than treating
weak closure as a synonym for strong conservation. The companion checks
a concrete nonzero-C example and its Jacobi identity exactly.

## 3. A regular point of the actual H_+, with a nonzero source and torque

Take the actual finite periodic free scalar, the actual TT Ward stress,
and exactly the chosen positive reduced Hamiltonian

    H_g=H_0+g sum_alpha Q_alpha T_alpha(phi)
                +g^2 sum_alpha T_alpha(phi)^2/(2 ell_alpha).

Retain the scalar uniform coordinate and delete only the homogeneous
gravity coordinates as in the existing model. The seed J_0,i is the
total centered-difference momentum, including its TT contribution. Its
three components commute and commute with H_0. All functions are smooth
polynomials in this finite phase space, so (5) is applicable.

Use first the cubic 3^3 lattice at a=1 and m^2=1. Let

    f=(1,-1,0), a=(1,2,-1), h=(1,0,-1),
    phi_*(x,y,z)=f(x)a(z)+h(y),    pi_*=0.              (12)

Choose normalized real cosine/sine TT modes with angle k=(0,0,2pi/3),
polarization diag(1,-1,0), eigenvalue ell=3:

    w_c(x,y,z)=cos(2pi z/3) diag(1,-1,0)/(3 sqrt(3)),
    w_s(x,y,z)=sin(2pi z/3) diag(1,-1,0)/(3 sqrt(3)).

They are orthonormal, tracefree and transverse. Their supported components
carry no hidden staggered phase because k_x=k_y=0. At z_* set

    Q_c=P_c=1, Q_s=P_s=0,
    all other TT coordinates and momenta zero.          (13)

The other modes are not removed from the model. Equations below evaluate
the full Hamiltonian and its full total momenta at this phase-space point.

Scalar momenta vanish, and P_TT=Q_TT makes every skew centered momentum
bilinear vanish. Thus all three J_0,i are zero. The three scalar momentum
derivatives of J_0 are -D_i^c phi_*. Their exact Gram matrix is

    Gram(D_i^c phi_*) = diag(27,27/2,21),
    det Gram = 15309/2 != 0.                            (14)

Therefore rank dJ_0=3 even before using any TT tangent directions. The
P_c derivative of every J_0,i is zero at this point, whereas
partial_(P_c) H_0=1. Hence rank d(H_0,J_0)=4 and dH_0!=0.

The actual Ward source has the exact identity

    sigma_xx-sigma_yy=(D_x^c phi)^2-(D_y^c phi)^2.

Direct evaluation of the original Ward frontend gives

    T_c(phi_*)=-3 sqrt(3)/4,    T_s(phi_*)=9/4.          (15)

The TT centered z momentum of this mode pair is

    J_TT,z=(sqrt(3)/2)(Q_c P_s-Q_s P_c).

Both pieces must be kept in the first-vertex torque. Their values are

    {J_scalar,z,H_1}(z_*)=9 sqrt(3)/4,
    {J_TT,z,H_1}(z_*)=-9 sqrt(3)/8,
    {J_total,z,H_1}(z_*)=9 sqrt(3)/8 != 0.              (16)

No omitted TT mode contributes at this point: centered differentiation
preserves the occupied cosine/sine mode pair. This is a nontrivial
interaction point, not a region where the old momentum already works.

Moreover the ACTUAL full Hamiltonian obeys

    dot Q_c(z_*)=1,
    dot P_c(z_*)=-3+3 sqrt(3) g/4.                     (17)

The first identity is exact for every g because the chosen H_+ has the
unshifted P^2 kinetic term. Its Q_c derivative gives the second; R depends
only on phi. Thus Sigma={Q_c=1} is a concrete common transverse section.
Take a small region with P_c>1/2. On a still smaller neighborhood the
flowbox and the coupling-parameter construction apply for |g|<delta.
Only bounded physical flow segments within that neighborhood are used.

The local correction is demonstrably nonvacuous. Because K(g,z_*)=0, its
first coefficient K_1,z vanishes at z_*, but its derivative along the free
flow is forced by conservation to be

    {K_1,z,H_0}(z_*)=-9 sqrt(3)/8.                     (18)

Along the short free orbit through z_* it is exactly the integral of the
negative first-vertex torque, with zero initial value. Equation (18) is
its nonzero initial slope, not a claimed global periodic primitive.

### The same regular point on a strict 6^3 lattice

Repeat all three profiles in (12) with period three on 6^3. Normalize w_c,
w_s by 6 sqrt(6) instead of 3 sqrt(3), again set (13), and keep m^2=1.
An independent exact finite sum gives

    Gram=diag(216,108,168),   det Gram=3919104,
    T_c=-3 sqrt(6)/2,        T_s=9 sqrt(2)/2,
    {J_total,z,H_1}=9 sqrt(6)/4 != 0,
    dot Q_c=1,              dot P_c=-3+3 sqrt(6)g/2.    (19)

Thus the local theorem applies on the same regulator class covered by
Round12's global strong obstruction. There is no contradiction: a small
open flowbox is not a neighborhood containing a full recurrent torus.

## 4. Why local construction is not a global completion

Equation (5) integrates a transport equation from one chosen section. To
extend a globally single-valued strong primitive around a closed orbit,
the integral of its forcing over the orbit must vanish. On a compact
invariant torus, integrating the transport equation against invariant Haar
measure gives the analogous necessary condition. Those are real global
compatibility conditions, absent on a short flowbox interval.

Likewise a global canonical conjugacy (2) would identify whole recurrent
structures, periods and return maps. A local noncritical straightening
theorem does not supply that. Following different paths through overlapping
charts can produce nontrivial transition maps or monodromy. The local
construction does not furnish a convergent global cover with consistent
constraint identifications.

For WEAK constraints, however, a return map need only preserve the zero
set and its coisotropic characteristic distribution. It may act by an
invertible matrix on local defining functions. Its transport/normal
holonomy need not be the identity. Therefore the strong orbit-average
obstruction cannot simply be renamed a weak obstruction. To exclude a
global weak surface for the actual model one must separately analyze
invariant coisotropic surfaces or the corresponding return-map/normal
equations. This note neither proves nor refutes that separate result.
The separate [Round13 regular-torus proof](../weak-constraints-round13/PROOF.md)
now supplies that additional argument for the fixed actual Hamiltonian and
seed. Its explicit action-independent normal flow is essential; the generic
logical counterexample below remains valid.

## 5. Explicit positive counterexample to strong-no-go implies weak-no-go

This section is a different, fully specified Hamiltonian, used only to
disprove the logical inference. It is not a replacement for actual H_+.
On T*S1_(theta,E) x T*R_(Q,P), optionally times two extra canonical pairs
(q2,p2),(q3,p3), take

    I=E+g QP,
    H_g=I^2/2=E^2/2+g E QP+g^2 Q^2 P^2/2 >= 0.        (20)

The globally defined regular constraints

    J=(P,p2,p3)

commute exactly and define a coisotropic surface. They satisfy

    {P,H_g}=-g(E+gQP)P,  {p2,H_g}={p3,H_g}=0.          (21)

Thus that surface is globally weakly invariant for every g. The seed is
regular, and near E=1 the free Hamiltonian has no critical point.

Nevertheless no globally periodic smooth strong deformation
K=P+g K1+O(g^2) exists on a neighborhood of the full theta circle and
the regular surface. The first equation is

    {K1,H_0}=-{P,H_1},
    E partial_theta K1=E P,
    partial_theta K1=P       (E != 0).                 (22)

Integrating over the theta circle would give 0=2pi P, impossible for
arbitrarily small nonzero P in any such open neighborhood. This excludes
arbitrary smooth K1, not just polynomial choices.

On a single angular chart there IS an exact strong repair,

    K=P exp(g theta),

and the full local canonical transformation is

    theta'=theta,  E'=E+gQP,
    Q'=Q exp(-g theta),  P'=P exp(g theta).             (23)

It follows either from its canonical brackets or from
E dtheta+P dQ=E' dtheta'+P' dQ'. The transformed Hamiltonian is E'^2/2.
But P' acquires exp(2pi g) on going around the circle, so it does not
define the required global strong momentum for real g!=0. The zero set
P'=0 DOES glue globally: it is just P=0. Weak closure allows exactly
that nontrivial scaling of the defining function.

This example has a positive Hamiltonian, a positive quartic completion,
regular first-class seed constraints, local strong repairs, a global
strong-deformation obstruction, and an exact global weak surface. It
shows why a weak no-go needs an additional argument, even when compact
recurrent free orbits are present. It does not contradict a separately
proved weak obstruction for the different actual H_+.

## 6. Physical and verification boundaries

- Equations (2)--(7) are exact for small finite g on a finite-dimensional
  patch, not merely a formal power series. Their sizes and derivative
  bounds can depend on the chosen point, lattice and section; no uniform
  cutoff/volume or long-time estimates are supplied.
- The functions K and J depend on an arbitrary transverse section and on
  actual-flow integrations. They are not derived local spatial Noether
  currents. Calling their seed homogeneous does not restore deleted
  homogeneous gravity coordinates or identify a physical receiver.
- Imposing a newly chosen coisotropic surface changes which states and
  equivalences are declared physical. Existence of that mathematical
  surface is not proof that the original reduced model possessed this
  gauge interpretation. A local leaf quotient is not a globally completed
  gravitational phase space or quantum physical Hilbert space.
- No quantum-unitary implementation, joint operator domain, empirical
  prediction, RH result or complete TOE closure is claimed.

`local_weak_check.py` reads the original Ward frontend, verifies the exact
3^3 and 6^3 regular/source/torque witnesses, checks the complete positive
logical counterexample and all 48 brackets of its local canonical map,
and checks a nonzero weak structure coefficient plus Jacobi. It uses
exact SymPy arithmetic only. The all-small-g flowbox theorem is proved
above, not inferred from numerical trajectories or finite CCR matrices.
